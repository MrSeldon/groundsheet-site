Radon · conditional insert

The county's radon zone, the state's indoor survey and the uranium under it, and why none of them is a measurement of this house

Radon is the one finding in the report that a test kit settles for thirty dollars. The insert appears when the county is EPA Zone 1 or 2 and gives three county-wide numbers, the EPA prediction, the state indoor survey and the USGS surface-uranium model, so a buyer knows why to test, not whether this house has radon. Santa Cruz County is Zone 2, with a 1.8 pCi/L median in 342 tested homes.

What the figures show

The first figure is the EPA's map of radon zones, one fill per county across about 300 miles around the subject county, with the subject county named. It is drawn at the resolution it has: county, no finer. The EPA assigned each county a zone in 1993 from aeroradiometric uranium surveys, soil permeability, geology and the indoor measurements then available: Zone 1 predicts an indoor average of 4 pCi/L or more, Zone 2 of 2 to 4, Zone 3 of under 2. The second page explains what the zone means at the address and prints the state's indoor survey for the county as a table: median, share at or above 4, number of homes tested, the years. The third page is the surface-uranium map: the USGS NURE airborne gamma-ray survey, flown in the 1970s and early 1980s on lines 5 to 10 km apart over about 99 percent of the lower 48, modelled by the USGS in 2020 into a grid of points about 2 km apart and drawn here on a 1 km grid so the grain reads as texture, light to dark from 0.4 to 2.7 ppm equivalent uranium, hatched where never flown or sea.

For Santa Cruz County the three numbers point the same way. Zone 2, the middle class. A state survey median of 1.8 pCi/L across 342 homes, with 8.2 percent at or above the action level. And 1.26 ppm eU at the point, low, under the national median of 1.6: the ground here carries relatively little of the uranium that feeds indoor radon. None of that is a reading from the house.

What it cannot show

Radon varies house to house on the same street. Two adjacent homes with different foundations, ages and ventilation can test at 1 and 6 pCi/L, and in a Zone 2 county the upper tail of that spread regularly crosses the action level. A county zone is a prediction of the county average; a county median is the middle of the homes that chose to test, a spread and not a rate for any street. The NURE lines were kilometres apart and the model is a smoothed surface. A Phase I environmental site assessment does not test for radon either; it is a records review with no air sampling. The only thing that tells you this house's number is a test in this house.

Why it matters to a buyer

Radon is a colourless, odourless radioactive gas from the decay of uranium and radium in soil and rock; it enters through slab cracks, sump pits and the foundation perimeter and accumulates indoors. It is the second-leading cause of lung cancer in the United States after tobacco and the leading cause among non-smokers. The decision is cheap and the fix is bounded: a short-term kit before closing, a 90-day test if it reads high, and a sub-slab vent system, usually 800 to 2,500 dollars installed, if the long test confirms it. The Groundsheet scale weights radon at 2 percent from the county zone; the weight is small because the zone is coarse, not because radon is.

What you can do

  1. Test before closing: a short-term kit in the lowest lived-in level with the house closed, 2 to 7 days; the state programme and hardware stores sell them for about 15 to 30 dollars. cdph.ca.gov
  2. At or above 4 pCi/L, follow with a 90-day test; that is the number a mitigation decision rests on. epa.gov
  3. If the long test confirms it, hire a certified mitigator for a sub-slab vent system and make it a condition of the sale. epa.gov
  4. Ask the seller for any past radon test or mitigation record; a fan already in place needs its own check. epa.gov

Reading this section

pCi/L
Picocuries per litre of air, the US radon unit; about 37 becquerels per cubic metre.
Rn-222
Radon-222, the gas from uranium-238 decaying via radium in soil and rock.
EPA Zones 1, 2, 3
County classes by predicted indoor average: 4 pCi/L or more; 2 to 4; under 2. The 1993 base dataset, a screening reference.
4 pCi/L
The EPA action level: fix a home at or above it; consider fixing from 2 pCi/L.
Short-term test kit
Left 2 to 7 days with the house closed (windows shut, no outdoor-air fans).
Alpha-track detector
A long-term detector, 90 days or more, averaging seasons into an annual estimate.
Sub-slab depressurization
A pipe and fan under the slab venting soil gas above the roof; the standard mitigation.
NRPP · NRSB
National Radon Proficiency Program and National Radon Safety Board, the certifiers of testers and mitigators.
State survey
The state radon program's aggregate of indoor tests for the county: median, share above 4, homes tested (n), years.
NURE · eU
National Uranium Resource Evaluation, the USGS airborne gamma-ray survey of the 1970s and early 1980s; eU is equivalent uranium in parts per million: low under 1.5, moderate 1.5 to 3.0, elevated over 3.0.
CBF
Census Cartographic Boundary Files, the 1:500k county outlines on the zone map.
Phase I ESA
Environmental Site Assessment under ASTM E1527: a records review; no air sampling, so it cannot resolve radon.
