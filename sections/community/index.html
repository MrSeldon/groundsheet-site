§ 08 · Community context

Schools, clinics, historic listings, and the one kind of airport that triggers a PFAS screen

Four kinds of neighbour that change what a finding means. A school or clinic within a mile is a sensitive receptor; a National Register listing can bring federal review to a project; and an airport where firefighting foam was used, within three miles, is the screening trigger for PFAS in groundwater. The Tampa sample has all four: 11 schools, 4 clinics, 8 listings, and Peter O. Knight airport 2.5 mi south with its three-mile arc reaching the property.

What the figure shows

The map places every receptor within a mile and every aviation facility within five on a paper-toned basemap, each kind with its own glyph so the plate reads without colour: a schoolhouse for a school, a pram for a daycare, a cross for a clinic (solid for a hospital), a columned building for a National Register listing, an airplane for an airport and a small grey H in a ring for a heliport. The nearest of each kind is numbered to a ledger with name, source record, distance and bearing. The one line that carries a finding is the dashed amber arc: the three-mile PFAS screening radius drawn around each AFFF-capable airport, and whether it reaches the subject. The caption under the map is generated from the same counts the key prints, so the words and the marks cannot disagree.

Tampa shows a dense downtown: 11 schools and 4 clinics within a mile, 8 Register listings including the Kress building 70 ft east and the Tampa Theatre, 7 rooftop heliports and a seaplane base with no screening radius, and Peter O. Knight airport 2.5 mi south, a runway airport where foam was plausibly used, whose arc reaches the property. Aptos shows a village: two schools, one listing (the Bayview Hotel, 350 ft north), one heliport with no radius, and no foam-capable airport within five miles.

What it cannot show

OpenStreetMap is a volunteer map, so a school missing from it is missing here. State daycare licence lists are not queried, so licensed home daycares will be absent. The FAA list includes every rooftop helipad; only runway facilities carry a screening radius. Register listings are points at the listed address, not district outlines, so a historic district's boundary is not drawn. And the PFAS arc is a screen, not a measurement: an airport inside three miles is a potential source, not a measured release; whether the chemicals are in the water under this lot is known only from a test of that water.

Why it matters to a buyer

A school, daycare, hospital or clinic is a sensitive receptor: near a regulated facility it tightens state cleanup targets and the questions a lender asks. A Register listing or district can bring Section 106 review to any work with a federal permit or federal money, which adds time, not a veto. The PFAS screen matters most on a private well or a shallow aquifer: the foam was sprayed for decades in runway fire drills, the two main compounds are now limited in drinking water to 4 parts per trillion, and since 2024 every utility's annual water quality report must carry its PFAS results. The Groundsheet scale weights PFAS at 4 percent on the airport screen alone, which is why Tampa reads High there; the section's steps say that a tap-water or well test is how to know, and the report's wording is being aligned to say "potential source, not a measured release" everywhere it appears.

What you can do

  1. On a private or shallow well inside the arc, test for the two foam chemicals the federal rule limits to 4 parts per trillion. epa.gov
  2. On city water, read the utility's annual water quality report; since 2024 it must carry PFAS results. epa.gov
  3. If a test finds them, a tap or under-sink filter certified for PFAS is the agency's fix; change cartridges on schedule. epa.gov
  4. A Register listing is within a mile: federally permitted or funded work gets a Section 106 review; ask the state historic office early. achp.gov
  5. Where no foam-using airport is within five miles, as at Aptos, PFAS is not a screening trigger; on a private well a one-time test still settles it.

Reading this section

AFFF-capable
FAA site type A (airport) or S (seaplane base), a runway facility where aqueous film-forming foam was plausibly used in fire drills; only these are scored for PFAS.
PFAS
Per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances, the "forever chemicals"; PFOA and PFOS are limited in drinking water to 4 parts per trillion.
3 mi radius
The screening distance around an AFFF-capable airport; inside it, ask about groundwater PFAS.
Receptor
A place contamination could reach; schools, daycares, hospitals and clinics are the sensitive ones.
FAA site types
A airport · H heliport · S seaplane base · B balloonport · G gliderport · U ultralight field.
Daycare
An OSM feature tagged kindergarten or childcare; state licence lists are not queried in this edition.
National Register listing
A building, site, structure or object on the National Register of Historic Places, drawn as a point at its address.
Section 106
National Historic Preservation Act review of federally permitted or funded work that could affect a listed property.
OSM · Overpass
OpenStreetMap, the volunteer map, queried live through the Overpass API.
FAA NTAD
The FAA aviation facilities layer as published in the Bureau of Transportation Statistics' National Transportation Atlas Database.
1 mi, 5 mi
Straight-line search radii from the subject: receptors and listings 1 mi, aviation facilities 5 mi.
Bearing
Compass direction from the subject to the mark, in eight points.
