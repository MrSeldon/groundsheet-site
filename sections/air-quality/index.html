§ 09 · Air quality

A year of air at the nearest monitor, one square per day, with every bad day named

An annual average hides the week that mattered. The section draws every day of the year at the nearest EPA monitor as one square coloured by its AQI band, tests the monitor against the three-year standards, and names the fire behind every day over the daily limit. Santa Cruz in 2025: 347 good days of 360 reported, none over the limit, and one smoke episode in September 2023 traced to the Quarry fire.

What the figures show

Page A opens with the almanac: the monthly mean AQI for the year as a line with the lowest and highest day of each month, against the two years before, and four stats that test the monitor against the standards. Below it, every episode: each day over 35.4 µg/m³ in three years gets a line with its peak, its AQI band and the fires of 100 acres or more active within 300 km in the days either side, named by size, so a smoke day has a cause. Then the hour's reading from AirNow at the time the report ran, stated as one hour's reading and nothing more. Page B is the calendar: one square per day, coloured by AQI band from the 24-hour PM2.5, months as rows, the monthly mean printed, the value printed on any day over 35.4, grey where no reading; then a table of the six bands with their day counts and the AQI and PM2.5 ranges, and the three-year table that the standards are actually judged on.

Santa Cruz is a clean monitor and the page says so without rounding it up: in 2025, 360 days reported, 347 Good, 13 Moderate, the worst day 12 µg/m³ on 15 March, both PM2.5 standards met and ozone well under. The one event in three years is 19 to 24 September 2023, when the Quarry fire about 128 miles north-east pushed one day to 47.4 µg/m³, AQI 130. Tampa's monitor at Munro Street reported 278 good days of 359 in 2025, which is what a city on a bay with a port looks like.

What it cannot show

One monitor speaks for the air at that monitor. A house in a canyon, beside a highway, downwind of a quarry or on a ridge above an inversion will differ, and the section cannot see that. EPA's attainment designations cover whole areas, not one site; a monitor that meets the standards does not make the region attain them. The AQS record is certified and lags by months, which is why the latest year can be incomplete; the AirNow reading is live and uncertified. And the whole section is about outdoor air: radon, mould and a gas stove are indoor questions.

Why it matters to a buyer

Nothing in this section triggers a permit or a disclosure; it matters for occupants sensitive to fine particles (children, the elderly, anyone with asthma or heart disease) and for anyone deciding how seriously to take fire season. The AQI's 100 line is where sensitive people cut outdoor effort; a house in a county with twenty days a year over it is a different house from one with none. The calendar also shows the shape of a year: in Santa Cruz the Moderate days cluster in winter inversions and the fire smoke comes in September. The Groundsheet scale weights air quality at 5 percent from the hour's AQI and the year's exceedance days.

What you can do

  1. Check the forecast and the fire-and-smoke map on a bad day and sign up for the county's alerts; the 100 line is where sensitive people cut outdoor effort. fire.airnow.gov
  2. On a smoke day close the windows and run the central fan on recirculate with a filter rated 13 or higher on the furnace-filter scale; most systems take one. epa.gov
  3. A portable air cleaner sized for the bedroom makes one clean room; the agency's guide says what size to buy and to avoid ozone generators. epa.gov
  4. Pair this with the wildfire page: smoke reaches further than flame, and the fires behind the worst days are named on the section's first page.

Reading this section

AQI
Air Quality Index, 0 to 500: 50 tops Good, 100 marks each pollutant's short-term standard; six bands, Good (0 to 50), Moderate (51 to 100), Unhealthy for sensitive groups (101 to 150), Unhealthy (151 to 200), Very unhealthy (201 to 300), Hazardous (301 to 500).
PM2.5 · O₃
Fine particles under 2.5 micrometres (AQS code 88101); ground-level ozone (44201).
µg/m³
Micrograms of fine particles per cubic metre of air; the PM2.5 bands run 0 to 9.0 Good, 9.1 to 35.4 Moderate, 35.5 to 55.4 Unhealthy for sensitive groups, 55.5 to 125.4 Unhealthy, 125.5 to 225.4 Very unhealthy, 225.5 and up Hazardous.
NAAQS
The national ambient air quality standards; both PM2.5 standards are three-year averages (annual mean 9, 98th-percentile day 35) and ozone's is the 70 ppb design value.
98th percentile
The value that 98 percent of the year's days fall at or under; the daily PM2.5 standard is tested on it.
Design value
For ozone, the fourth-highest daily 8-hour peak averaged over three years.
Episode
A day over 35.4 µg/m³ with its adjacent days over 9.0; fires of 100 acres or more within 300 km (186 mi), three days either side, named by size.
AQS · AirNow
EPA's certified monitor archive; EPA's hourly real-time feed, uncertified.
Site id
The AQS id: state (2 digits), county (3), site (4); 060870007 is California, Santa Cruz County, site 0007.
NIFC, MTBS
National Interagency Fire Center perimeters (WFIGS, with discovery and containment dates); Monitoring Trends in Burn Severity burned areas (ignition dates), used to name the fires behind smoke days.
