§ 03 · Wildfire

The hazard class at the parcel, and what has actually burned nearby

Two different questions, answered side by side. The US Forest Service's Wildfire Hazard Potential says how fire-prone the standing landscape is at the point; the burn perimeters say what has burned within reach since 2015. At Aptos the two disagree in an instructive way, class 2 of 5 at the parcel and 560,243 acres burned within reach, most of it in August 2020.

What the figure shows

Page A is the hazard: the US Forest Service's Wildfire Hazard Potential, a raster that models the relative likelihood of a parcel experiencing wildfire from fuels, terrain, weather and historical ignition patterns, in five classes from very low to very high, drawn over satellite imagery with the recent burn perimeters outlined on top. The class at the point is read from the pixel under the parcel. Page B is the history: the ten largest burns within reach since 2015 as a table (name, year, acres, distance to the nearest edge, bearing) and a locator that places every perimeter by its true position and extent relative to the property, numbers keyed to the table, no hazard shading, geometry only. Page C is the annex.

Aptos is the case that explains why both pages exist. The parcel sits in class 2 of 5, low: a coastal village pocket with little fuel. Yet the locator shows the CZU Lightning Complex, 85,839 acres in the Santa Cruz Mountains, with its nearest edge 11.1 miles to the west, and the SCU Lightning Complex, 405,644 acres, 27.1 miles to the north-east; 23 perimeters and 560,243 acres in all. The rating describes the standing landscape; the history describes what has burned. A reader who sees only one of the two draws the wrong conclusion either way.

What it cannot show

The WHP is a probabilistic estimate of underlying potential at 270 m resolution, not a prediction, a forecast or a statement about this house's roof. Perimeters under 100 acres and fires before 2015 are not drawn, which keeps the map to consequential, well-documented events and leaves out the small grass fire next door. The NIFC record is incomplete before 2021, which is why MTBS burned-area boundaries are merged in, with their one-to-two-year lag. Prescribed and vegetation-management burns appear in the roster because they are mapped perimeters, not because they were wildfires; the table names them (VMP). Distances run to the nearest perimeter edge, so a 405,644-acre fire "27.1 mi away" is a fire whose nearest edge was 27.1 mi away. And the section says nothing about the one thing an insurer cares most about: whether the house has ember-resistant vents and a cleared five feet.

One more limit, stated plainly: the search radius is 50 km (31 mi) from the point, and the sample's caption says "thirty miles"; two of the 23 listed perimeters fall at 33.9 and 34.6 mi by nearest edge. The report team is aligning the caption and the filter for the next render; this page reports the sample as it printed.

Why it matters to a buyer

In California the wildfire question is three questions: the insurer's (can the house be insured, and at what price, which turns on the state's fire hazard severity zone and the house's hardening), the lender's (the same), and the household's (is there a way out, and what does the air do in September). The WHP class is the landscape view; the state's statutory zones, read on the California hazard zones page, are the disclosure view; the fire history is the memory that neighbours carry and maps forget. The Groundsheet scale weights wildfire at 4 percent from the WHP class, which is why a parcel in a low class pocket with a vast fire history still reads Low in this section and the history is kept on the page in words.

What you can do

  1. Keep the first five feet around the house free of anything that burns, then thin and space plants out to 100 feet: the defensible-space rule the state enforces. fire.ca.gov
  2. The cheapest hardening is ember-proofing: fine metal mesh on vents, clean gutters, no wood against the wall; the state fire marshal's low-cost retrofit list (Assembly Bill 38) is the checklist. osfm.fire.ca.gov
  3. Write a go-plan now: two ways out, a meeting point, the county alert sign-up, a go-bag packed through fire season. ready.gov
  4. Smoke reaches further than flame: pair this with the air quality steps and the fire-and-smoke map on a bad day. fire.airnow.gov
  5. Before the offer, get an insurance quote in writing; in a high or very high zone, ask the seller for the defensible-space inspection the state requires at sale.

Reading this section

WHP
Wildfire Hazard Potential, a USFS rating in five classes (very low to very high) of the relative likelihood a parcel experiences wildfire, modelled from fuels, terrain, weather and ignition history; a probabilistic estimate of potential, not a forecast.
Burn perimeter
The mapped outline of a past fire's footprint; the section screens perimeters of 100 acres or more since 2015 within reach of the property.
Recent fire history
The roster of qualifying burns over a fixed ten-year window; an empty roster is a genuine finding, not missing data.
Prescribed / VMP burn
A controlled fire set by land managers to reduce fuel; VMP is CAL FIRE's Vegetation Management Program. Listed because it is a mapped perimeter; it represents risk reduction, not damage.
NIFC
National Interagency Fire Center InterAgency Fire Perimeter History, the federal consolidated record of mapped fire footprints; incomplete before 2021.
MTBS
Monitoring Trends in Burn Severity: every fire over 1,000 acres in the West and 500 in the East since 1984, from satellite imagery, with a one-to-two-year lag; merged with NIFC, deduped on name and year.
Probabilistic raster
The WHP is a grid of pixels each carrying a hazard value; the section reads the pixel at the property's coordinates, a parcel-class lookup.
Bearing notation
Eight-point compass beside each distance: "27.1 mi NE" means 27.1 miles to the north-east of the property, to the nearest edge of the perimeter.
CAL FIRE · FRAP
The California Department of Forestry and Fire Protection and its Fire and Resource Assessment Program, which maps the state responsibility area and the hazard severity zones read on the hazard zones page.
EPSG:3857 · WGS 84
Web Mercator on the WGS 84 datum.
