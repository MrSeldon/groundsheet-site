§ 04 insert · Karst and sinkholes · Florida

Whether the rock under the lot dissolves, and every sinkhole reported within five miles

Where carbonate rock is mapped at or near the surface, the geology section grows an insert. It names the rock under the lot and its age, maps every subsidence incident the state survey holds within five miles with the size and rate as filed, and sets out what Florida's sinkhole statute requires of insurers and sellers. Tampa: limestone of the Tampa Member underfoot, 30 reports within 5 mi, none within 1 mi, none verified by a geologist.

What the figures show

The map is drawn the way the wells map is, from above with rings at one and five miles over faded arterial roads, and the ground itself is the subject: the USGS polygon of carbonate rock at or near the surface is hatched across the frame, with buried and pseudokarst areas in a lighter pattern. Every subsidence incident report the Florida Geological Survey holds within the frame is a mark, sized by the width filed and shaped by the rate: a rapid collapse, a slow subsidence, or a rate not recorded. The nearest and the largest are labelled with their year. The second page lists every report within five miles nearest first, with its FGS reference, year, distance, bearing, size as filed (length by width by depth, in feet), rate, whether a licensed geologist verified it, whether damage was reported, how precisely it was placed (the parcel, a GPS fix, or a square of the survey grid) and the note as filed. Then what Florida law requires, the steps and the annex.

Under Tampa the rock is the Tampa Member of the Arcadia Formation, limestone with dolostone, sand and clay, 27 to 12 million years old, inside the USGS carbonate area; the FGS classes the ground as sinkhole type Area III, 30 to 200 ft of clayey cover over the rock, where sinkholes are most numerous and abrupt and cover-collapse dominates. Of the 30 reports within five miles, 12 were filed as rapid, 2 as slow and 16 without a rate; 6 reported damage; none is within a mile and none was verified. Aptos has no soluble rock mapped and no insert.

What it cannot show

A subsidence incident report is a call or a claim, placed to the parcel or to a square of the survey grid: where someone reported ground movement, not an exact spot and not a confirmed sinkhole. "Verified" means a licensed geologist confirmed one, and most reports were never checked; an Allstate claim from 1985 with no rate recorded is in the register beside a pothole a citizen reported in 2025. The register records what was reported, so a neighbourhood of diligent insurers shows more marks than one of stoic owners. The carbonate polygon is a national map at 1:1,000,000-class scale. Nothing on the page says whether a cavity lies under this lot; only ground-penetrating radar and borings to the limestone can.

Why it matters to a buyer

Karst is ground on rock that dissolves: limestone, dolomite, gypsum. Slightly acid rainwater widens cracks into channels and cavities over thousands of years, and the cover of sand and clay on top falls or sags into them. Two kinds of sinkhole follow. Cover-subsidence: sandy cover trickles slowly into the rock and a shallow bowl grows over years, cracking slabs and tilting floors. Cover-collapse: clayey cover bridges a cavity until it cannot and the ground opens in hours, the kind that reaches the news. Florida Statute 627.706 makes every property insurer cover catastrophic ground cover collapse, a defined event with four parts (an abrupt collapse, a depression visible to the naked eye, structural damage including the foundation, and the building condemned), all of which must hold; a cracked slab or a dip in the yard does not qualify. Insurers must also offer sinkhole loss cover, for structural damage from sinkhole activity, as an option with its own premium and a deductible of 1, 2, 5 or 10 percent. A paid, unrepaired sinkhole claim follows the address and is often uninsurable, which is why the seller's answer matters.

What you can do

  1. Order a sinkhole inspection to the statutory standard: ground-penetrating radar across the lot and borings to the limestone, by a licensed geologist or engineer. leg.state.fl.us
  2. Ask the seller in writing whether a sinkhole claim was ever made on the property and whether the money went to repair; the statute requires the answer and the insurer files the report with the county clerk. leg.state.fl.us
  3. Quote the sinkhole-loss endorsement before the offer (the standard policy covers only catastrophic ground cover collapse) and keep every repair record. myfloridacfo.com
  4. Report new ground movement to the state survey; it keeps the incident register this map draws from. floridadep.gov

Karst

Carbonate rock
Limestone and dolomite, calcium or magnesium carbonate; slightly acid rainwater dissolves it along cracks, which makes karst.
Cover-collapse
The sand or clay over a cavity bridges it until it cannot, then falls in within hours; the damaging kind.
Cover-subsidence
Sandy cover trickles slowly into the dissolved rock and the surface sags over years: a bowl, not a hole.
