§ 01 · Facility proximity

Which regulated facilities sit within two miles, and which of them matter

Every EPA-regulated facility within two miles of the point, on a survey plate with rings at a quarter, a half, one and two miles. The count is rarely the finding; the tier is. Tampa's 708 facilities include seven that warrant a closer look; Aptos's 263 include none.

What the figure shows

The plate is a survey of every facility the EPA regulates within two miles of the point, placed at its registered coordinates on a muted ground with no basemap, so the rings and the marks carry the reading. Four rings at a quarter, a half, one and two miles give concentric counts, so you see how the density falls off with distance and not just the total. Each facility is drawn by its tier: a numbered callout for Tier II, keyed to the table on the next page, with a tick per medium its latest TRI submission released to (air, water, land); a small mark for Tier III, the ordinary permit holders; a dot for Tier IV, registrations with no release or enforcement record. The crosshair at the centre is the subject; every distance and bearing in the section is measured from it. The wind rose in the margin orients the plate to true north and, where a station reports, prints the prevailing wind and the calm share (Tampa: north-east 9 percent, calm 13 percent, from station KTPA), so a reader can see which facilities sit upwind.

The Tampa sample shows what the section is for. 708 facilities within two miles sounds alarming; the plate says that 369 hold standard permits and 332 are registration-only, that seven are Tier II (large-quantity generators of hazardous waste or Toxics Release Inventory reporters), clustered about 1.2 miles to the east, and that none is Tier I. Composition, not volume, is the lens. Aptos shows the other case: 263 facilities, 261 of them standard permits and two registrations, no Tier I or II at all, the nearest six a medical office, an equipment yard, a tyre shop, a dry cleaner and two garages within a tenth of a mile.

What it cannot show

Registration is not release. A facility on the list handles regulated material under a permit, which is what the list records; a dry cleaner with a RCRA registration and no violations is a dry cleaner doing it properly. Proximity is screened as a proxy for the three physical routes a release could travel to the point, air, water and land; the plate flags what warrants a closer look and does not model dispersion, groundwater flow or exposure. The ECHO record is federal: a state-only cleanup site, a historical spill never entered, or a facility registered at a head-office address rather than its yard will be missing or misplaced. Distances run to the registered coordinate, which for a large site may be its gate. And the tier scheme is Groundsheet's own reading of EPA's designations, not an EPA classification; the raised k after a tier in the report points to the key that says so.

Why it matters to a buyer

Two things change when a Tier I or II facility is close. The first is money: a lender or an insurer that sees a Superfund site, a leaking tank or a significant violator within reach can ask for a Phase I environmental site assessment before closing, and a Phase I is a few thousand dollars and a few weeks. The second is water: on a private well, or where the ground is shallow and sandy, what a facility handles is what a water test should look for. In the Groundsheet scale this is the one weighted section: each facility's program weight, times a distance factor, summed; permit volume alone cannot lift it past Moderate, so a village full of garages reads Moderate and stays there, while one leaking tank at 0.2 mi reads High on its own.

What you can do

  1. Read the table on page B of the section: the Tier I and II facilities nearest first, each with its designation, compliance status, distance, bearing and elevation difference, so you know whether it sits uphill of the lot. Look each one up by name in EPA ECHO for its inspection and violation history.
  2. On a private well, or on a shallow aquifer, test once for what the nearest Tier II facilities handle; the TRI chemical lines in the report name them. The EPA's private well guidance says how to pick a certified lab.
  3. If a Tier I facility is within reach, or the composite reads Elevated or High, ask your agent to make a Phase I ESA a contingency; the report's method page explains why a Groundsheet is not one.
  4. For a Tier III cluster like Aptos Village, the practical step is the seller's own disclosure of any tank, spill or cleanup on the parcel, and the state's cleanup database (in California, GeoTracker) for the neighbours.

Reading this section

Tier I to IV
Groundsheet's own four-tier scheme, not an EPA classification. Tier I (critical): Superfund NPL, LUST, RCRA treatment-storage-disposal, significant non-compliance. Tier II (elevated): RCRA large-quantity generators and TRI reporters. Tier III (standard): all other RCRA-permitted facilities. Tier IV (registration): FRS registrations with no release or enforcement record.
NPL
National Priorities List, the Superfund sites; one within 0.5 mi sets the Severe level by itself.
LUST
Leaking underground storage tank, an EPA designation with its own section and weight.
RCRA-TSD
A hazardous waste treatment, storage and disposal facility under the Resource Conservation and Recovery Act.
SNC
Significant non-complier, a facility under active EPA enforcement.
RCRA
Resource Conservation and Recovery Act; RCRAInfo generator tiers (LQG, SQG, VSQG, TSD) drive the Tier II classification for large generators.
TRI
Toxics Release Inventory: annual per-chemical release submissions; the sparkline under a reporter's chemical line shows its releases by year against the sector median.
EPA ECHO
Enforcement and Compliance History Online, the federal source of record for facility compliance status, inspections and program flags across RCRA, CAA, CWA, SDWA and CERCLA.
Exposure pathway
Air (emissions and drift), water (runoff and groundwater), land (soil and waste handling): the three routes proximity stands in for; Tier II callouts carry a tick per medium released.
Bearing
Eight-point compass from the subject to the facility plus the degrees clockwise from true north.
Δ elev
Elevation difference, facility minus subject, in feet on NAVD 88 from USGS 3DEP; positive means the facility is uphill.
Status
EPA ECHO's compliance snapshot: significant violator, active enforcement, or in compliance; the inspected date is the latest formal EPA inspection on record.
R.F., scale
The representative fraction at plate centre and a graphic scale bar in statute miles that stays true if the page is resized.
EPSG:3857 · WGS 84
Web Mercator projection on the WGS 84 datum, the standard for web and satellite basemaps.
