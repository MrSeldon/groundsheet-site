§ 10 · Wind and storms

Every hurricane track within fifty miles and tornado path within ten, with the tract's wind rating, and why none of it describes this roof

History is the area's, not the house's. The section draws seventy-five years of storm centres and tornado paths around the point and prints FEMA's wind rating for the census tract; what the building does in the next storm depends on its roof, its straps and its openings, which only an inspector can see. Tampa: 31 cyclones within 50 mi since 1950, six as hurricanes when nearest, 77 tornado paths within 10 mi, the nearest 0.6 mi.

What the figures show

The map draws every tropical cyclone whose centre passed within fifty miles since 1950 as a track weighted by its category when nearest the subject, labelled with the storm's name, year, category and closest approach; tropical storms lighter, depressions and remnants dashed. Tornado paths within ten miles are drawn as the straight line from the SPC start point to the end point, weighted by scale. Rings at 10 and 50 miles, the subject at the centre, land and water from Natural Earth. Page B lists the storms nearest first with their date, category and wind when nearest, peak wind, distance and bearing; then the tornado paths with date, scale, distance, length, width and injuries; then what an insurer asks, the steps and the annex.

Tampa reads as the record says: 31 tracks within fifty miles since 1950, six at hurricane strength when nearest, none at Category 3 or more. The nearest at hurricane strength was Irma in 2017, Category 1 at 20 mi east-north-east; the strongest to pass was Milton in 2024, Category 2 at 28 mi to the south-south-east. Fourteen more passed at hurricane strength before 1950, the nearest in 1946. Tornadoes: 325 paths within 25 miles, the strongest an F4 on 4 April 1966 that ran 136 miles and passed 3.4 mi north-north-west, hurting 530 and killing 11. FEMA's National Risk Index rates the tract Relatively High for hurricane and tornado.

What it cannot show

The best track is each storm's centre every six hours: the 31 tracks are centres that passed, not winds that blew here, and a hurricane's damaging winds reach 25 to 50 miles out from the centre. "Category when nearest" is interpolated between the two fixes either side of the closest approach. A tornado path is a straight line between two surveyed points; the real track wandered. The NRI's expected annual loss is the tract's average yearly dollar loss from one hazard, the neighbourhood's exposure and not this building's. ASCE 7's wind-speed maps, the ones the building code actually uses, are served only through interactive tools with no public data feed, so the NRI rating stands in and the annex says so. And nothing on the page describes the roof.

Why it matters to a buyer

In Florida the wind question is priced by the house, not the street. A wind mitigation inspection on the uniform form (OIR-B1-1802, valid five years) records the roof's shape, nailing, straps, water barrier and opening protection, and the statute makes each a premium discount; a four-point inspection is asked for on homes over about twenty years old. Expect a hurricane deductible of 2 to 10 percent of the dwelling limit, and ask whether the carrier is Citizens, the state's insurer of last resort. Florida's statewide code since 2002 takes its wind loads from ASCE 7, with a wind-borne debris region along the coast where glazed openings must be impact-rated or shuttered; a house permitted before 2002 was built to the county code of its day, so the year built and the roof's permit date say more than the address. The section appears where a tornado of EF2 or more has passed within 10 miles or a hurricane within 50; Aptos does not trigger it.

What you can do

  1. Find your evacuation zone and nearest shelter before the season; zones follow surge, not distance from the water. floridadisaster.org
  2. Order a wind mitigation inspection (form OIR-B1-1802): roof deck, straps, shape and opening protection, each a discount by law. floir.com
  3. Protect every opening with impact-rated shutters or glazing; the garage door first, it fails first. ready.gov
  4. When the roof is replaced ask for a sealed deck and straps; the Fortified Roof standard is the one insurers recognise. fortifiedhome.org
  5. Pick a tornado room now: an interior room on the lowest floor, or a safe room built to FEMA P-320. fema.gov

Reading this section

HURDAT2
NOAA National Hurricane Center best track: every Atlantic (1851 on) and northeast Pacific (1949 on) cyclone's centre, wind and status every six hours.
SPC
NOAA Storm Prediction Center; its tornado database runs from 1950 with a start and an end point for each tornado.
Tornado path
The straight line from the SPC start point to the end point; a record with no end point is drawn as a dot.
NRI
FEMA National Risk Index: a census-tract rating for 18 hazards, Very Low to Very High, from expected annual loss, social vulnerability and resilience.
Expected annual loss
NRI's average yearly dollar loss in the tract from one hazard; the tract's exposure, not this building's.
Saffir-Simpson
Hurricane categories by one-minute sustained wind: 1 is 74 to 95 mph, 2 is 96 to 110, 3 is 111 to 129, 4 is 130 to 156, 5 is 157 or more; TS a tropical storm, 39 to 73; TD a depression, under 39.
When nearest
The category where the track came nearest the subject, from the wind interpolated between the fixes either side.
EF scale
Enhanced Fujita tornado damage scale since 2007: EF0 65 to 85 mph, EF1 86 to 110, EF2 111 to 135, EF3 136 to 165, EF4 166 to 200, EF5 over 200; F0 to F5 is the older Fujita scale, printed before 2007.
ASCE 7
The load standard the building codes adopt; its wind-speed maps have no public data feed, so the NRI loss stands in.
Wind-borne debris region
ASCE 7 and the Florida Building Code: within 1 mi of the coast where the design wind speed is 130 mph or more, or anywhere 140 mph or more; glazed openings must be impact-rated or shuttered.
Wind mitigation inspection
Florida's uniform form OIR-B1-1802 recording the features that resist wind; valid five years; each feature earns a discount under s. 627.0629.
4-point inspection
An insurer's check of roof, electrical, plumbing and heating and cooling on older homes; not a wind inspection.
