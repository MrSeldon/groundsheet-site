Insert · California hazard zones · Civil Code 1103

The six zones a California seller must disclose, read from the public maps for the point, each Yes or No with the nearest edge

Every California sale carries a Natural Hazard Disclosure Statement answering six questions from six public maps. This insert reads the same maps for the point and prints the answer, the nearest edge and the map's own date for each, so a buyer can see the disclosure coming and check it. Aptos: outside all six, the state responsibility area's edge 0.02 mi away and the flood zone 0.09 mi.

What the figures show

The first page is a table and a map. The table lists the six zones in the statute's own words, with Yes or No for the point, the nearest edge in miles and a sixteen-point bearing when the answer is No, the map it was read from with that map's own date, and the day the service answered. Two more rows split the state fire map into its High and Very High zones, because the disclosure form asks about the local map's very high zone and the state area separately and a buyer wants both. The map draws every zone polygon within five miles with its distance from the subject, rings at one and five miles, arterial roads and water for orientation, no basemap. The second page reads the map out zone by zone (how many polygons of each kind within five miles and the nearest), gives one line on what each zone means for a buyer, and lists the official lookups.

For Aptos the answer is No six times, with the edges close enough to matter: the state responsibility area's edge 0.02 mi north-north-east (the lot itself is in the local area, classed non-wildland on the 2025 local map), the Zone AE floodplain 0.09 mi south-west, the state map's High fire zone 0.63 mi north, the local Very High zone 5.0 mi east, the Watsonville West fault zone 5.5 mi east, the Newell dam inundation area 6.2 mi west, and the nearest seismic hazard zone 10.2 mi north on the Los Gatos quad. No fault zone or seismic hazard zone map has been released for the lot's own quadrangle, which means no zones there, mapped or not.

What it cannot show

This is a reading of the maps, not a Natural Hazard Disclosure Statement, and does not replace one; the statement is a form the seller signs, usually filled by a disclosure company from the same maps, and the duty attaches to the form. The answer is for a point: a lot that straddles a zone edge can be partly inside, which only a parcel overlay settles, and at 0.02 mi from the state responsibility area that caveat is live for Aptos. The fire maps are adopted by local ordinance, which may add zones beyond the state's. A quad with no released map has no zones, mapped or not, which is a statement about the map and not about the ground. And where a service did not answer in three tries and no county tile was on hand, the row is left unanswered rather than marked No, and the headline counts only the zones that answered.

Why it matters to a buyer

The duty attaches only where the answer is Yes, and each Yes carries something concrete. Flood zone: flood insurance is mandatory on a federally backed mortgage, priced by the zone and an elevation certificate. Dam inundation: the lot lies in the path if the named dam failed; ask for the emergency action plan and the evacuation route; no insurance duty attaches. Very high fire zone, local area: 100 ft of defensible space, ember-resistant construction for new work, and the seller's fire-hardening and defensible-space disclosure under Civil Code 1102.19 (Assembly Bill 38). State responsibility area: CAL FIRE is the fire agency, with defensible space under Public Resources Code 4291 and, in a high or very high zone, a defensible-space inspection or the buyer's written agreement to get one before close. Earthquake fault zone: a fault investigation by an engineering geologist before any building for people, and a 50 ft setback from an active trace unless the study clears the site. Seismic hazard zone: a site-specific geotechnical report before a permit for new construction or a large addition, with mitigation if liquefaction or landslide is found. Seeing the six answers before the disclosure arrives turns a form into a checklist.

What you can do

  1. Ask the seller for the Natural Hazard Disclosure Statement and the disclosure report behind it; the report names the maps and dates it used. leginfo.legislature.ca.gov, Civil Code 1103.2
  2. Check the fire zone on the State Fire Marshal's viewer; in a high or very high zone ask for the defensible-space inspection and the fire-hardening list (Civil Code 1102.19). osfm.fire.ca.gov/fhsz
  3. Look up dam inundation maps on the Division of Safety of Dams viewer; the county emergency plan names the evacuation route. fmds.water.ca.gov/maps/damim
  4. Check fault and seismic hazard zones in the CGS earthquake zones application; where a zone is mapped, a geotechnical report is the parcel-level answer. maps.conservation.ca.gov/cgs
  5. Confirm the flood zone and order an elevation certificate at the FEMA Flood Map Service Center; the certificate sets the premium. msc.fema.gov

Reading this section

Civil Code 1103
The statute (sections 1103 to 1103.14) that makes a California seller disclose, on a set form, whether the property lies in any of six mapped hazard zones.
Natural Hazard Disclosure Statement
That form, usually filled by a disclosure company from the same public maps; this insert reads the maps, is not the form, and does not replace it.
Here, nearest edge
Yes when the subject point lies inside a mapped polygon; otherwise the miles and sixteen-point bearing to the nearest polygon's edge, searched to 25 mi.
Vintage, retrieved
The map's own date (FIRM panel, dam map approval, fire map adoption, quad zone release) and the day the service answered; "county tile" marks an earlier download used when it did not.
Special flood hazard area
The 1 percent annual chance floodplain (Zone A or V with their letters); row 1 reuses the record printed in § 02.
Dam failure inundation
The area an engineer modelled as flooded if a jurisdictional dam failed, approved by the Division of Safety of Dams for emergency plans, not insurance.
Fire hazard severity zone
The State Fire Marshal's map of expected fire behaviour from fuel, slope and weather: Moderate, High or Very High.
State responsibility area
Wildland where the state (CAL FIRE) is the fire agency; cities and populated county land are the local responsibility area.
2024 and 2025 fire maps
The state-area map took effect 1 Apr 2024; the local-area maps came in Feb and Mar 2025 and are adopted by local ordinance, which may add zones.
Assembly Bill 38
Civil Code 1102.19 (2019): in a high or very high zone the seller lists the home's fire-hardening features and shows a defensible-space inspection, or the buyer agrees to get one.
Defensible space
The cleared and thinned zone around a building, 100 ft or to the lot line: Public Resources Code 4291 (state area), Government Code 51182 (local very high zone).
Alquist-Priolo
The 1972 Earthquake Fault Zoning Act: a zone about 500 ft each side of a mapped active fault, where a fault study precedes building for people.
Seismic hazard zone
The 1990 Seismic Hazard Mapping Act zones: liquefaction (wet loose ground that loses strength in shaking) or earthquake-induced landslide (slopes that fail in shaking).
Quad
A 7.5-minute quadrangle, the sheet the state zones are released on; a quad with no released map has no zones, mapped or not.
Map not reached
No answer in three tries of 20 s and no county tile on hand; the row is unanswered, not No, and the headline counts only the zones that answered.
CGS · CAL FIRE · FRAP
The California Geological Survey; the Department of Forestry and Fire Protection; its Fire and Resource Assessment Program.
