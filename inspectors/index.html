The question in the driveway that the crawlspace cannot answer

"Is there anything near here I should worry about?" You hear it every week and the honest answer is that it is not in your scope. Groundsheet makes it an add-on you sell beside the inspection, at a wholesale price, delivered with your report.

The add-on model

Your inspection answers what the house is doing. Groundsheet answers what the ground and the surroundings are doing, from the public record: the regulated facilities within two miles and their status, the flood zone, the fire history, the wells on file, the radon zone, the listed species and wetlands, the pipelines, the schools and clinics, a year of air, and where it applies, the sinkholes and the California hazard zones. It is a separately identified document with its own cover, its own authorship line and its own limits, so that nobody mistakes it for a finding you made or a test you ran.

You offer it as an optional add-on when the inspection is booked. The client says yes or no. If yes, we generate it for the address and it goes out with your inspection report. The word "environmental" never attaches to your scope; the report says on its cover that it is a screening of public records and not an inspection, a test or a Phase I.

How delivery works

  1. Booking. When the inspection is booked, the client is offered the add-on. Yes or no. You collect the retail price with your fee, or we bill the client directly; both work.
  2. The address. You send us the address, or enter it on your Groundsheet page. We geocode it and confirm the point. Most reports finish in under ten minutes; you will have it before you leave for the site.
  3. Delivery. The PDF arrives in your inbox and at a private web link, with your name and company on the cover under "Presented by" and "Research and report by Groundsheet" beneath. Attach the PDF to your inspection report, or send the link. The client gets the same document either way.
  4. The bill. One monthly invoice at the wholesale price for the reports delivered that month. No subscription for inspectors during the pilot; the Office tier at $249 a month exists for franchises that want five seats and white label.

The Spectora path

Inspectors write their reports in software, mostly Spectora since it bought HomeGauge. There is no marketplace inside it where third parties list add-ons, so "listing it in the app" is not something we can do. What we can do is build a Groundsheet button inside Spectora with their cooperation, so that you order our report from the page where you write yours and it attaches itself to the inspection. That is the integration we are pursuing, after the first inspectors are delivering by email and link. If you use other software, tell us which; delivery by email and link works with all of them today.

What we ask of an inspector in the pilot

  • Offer it on real bookings for a month and tell us the yes rate.
  • Tell us what clients asked that it did not answer.
  • Tell us how it should be described in your booking flow so that nobody thinks environmental field testing was performed.
  • If your organisation has a vendor evaluation process, point us at it; we will go through it.
