The agent who knew about the creek is the agent who gets the referral

Your buyers ask you about the well, the fire, the plant across the highway. You know the house, not the ground. Groundsheet puts the ground on your cover, in your client's hands, in the contingency week.

The agent who knew

A buyer remembers two things about their agent: whether the offer won, and whether the agent knew something they did not. The agent who said "that lot backs onto a mapped wetland, so ask about the fence line before you plan the addition" before the inspector arrived is the agent the buyer's sister calls next spring.

Groundsheet is that knowledge in a form you can hand over. One address, sixteen sections, every map with its source and its date, the finding written before the topic, a codes key for every term. It is not a hazard score from a company you have never heard of; it is the public record, drawn well, with the limits printed next to the claims. Your buyer reads the cover in a minute, the summary in five, and knows which three questions to ask before the contingency expires.

The co-branded cover

With the membership, the cover carries a "Presented by" line: your name, licence number and brokerage. Below it, in the same strip, "Research and report by Groundsheet". The authorship line stays, the method stays, the sources stay; the client sees you brought it and the report stays independent. That separation is deliberate and counsel is reviewing it: the report is a research product you buy for your client, not a referral, and Groundsheet pays no fees to agents in any state.

The leave-behind

Two pages: the cover with the rating and the follow-up panel, and the summary with the sixteen sections on the ladder. Print it, leave it on the counter at the showing, hand it across the table. The full 40 pages are the PDF and the web page behind it, at a link you can send from your phone. The leave-behind is what an agent who knew looks like on paper.

The ten founding agents

We are looking for ten buyer's agents in Santa Cruz County to help us learn whether this is useful during the inspection contingency. Here is the ask, written out, so that there is nothing to read between the lines:

We would like to give you the membership free for six months and free reports on up to three live transactions. In return we ask for an hour of honest feedback after each report: what was useful, what was wrong, what your client asked that it did not answer. If the report earned it, we would welcome a sentence we can print with your name; if it did not, tell us why, and that is the whole obligation. There is no referral arrangement and no commitment to recommend Groundsheet to anyone. The report is supplemental information. It does not replace a home inspection, statutory disclosures, environmental testing or specialist advice, and we will say so on every cover.

Ten seats, in the order agents say yes, through 31 January 2027 or until the seats are filled. To take one, write to hello@getgroundsheet.com with your name, brokerage and the next address you have under contract, or use the contact page. We read every reply ourselves.

The rules we hold ourselves to

  • No real agent's name, licence or brokerage appears on a public mockup without written permission. The demonstration above uses a fictional agent and says so.
  • A private preview of a real listing with your name on it is made only after you have asked for it.
  • No referral fees, no commissions, no "preferred vendor" payments, in any state. Counsel is reviewing the RESPA and state questions before launch, and the draft terms say what the licence covers.
  • The report says what it is not on its cover: not a Phase I, not an inspection, not a disclosure, not a measurement.
