Two houses, eighty pages, every map sourced

A hillside house in Aptos, California, and a downtown lot in Tampa, Florida. Both reports are public in full so that you can judge the thing itself, not a description of it.

123 Post Office Dr, Aptos, CA 95003

Moderate, 2.9 of 10, led by seismic hazard and listed species. Two High sections inside a Moderate composite: the case for the follow-up panel on the cover. 40 pages, 24 sources, 176 codes keyed.

Why these two

They were chosen to be different. Aptos is a coastal hillside in a county with 35 listed species, a high seismic design category and a short fire history on its doorstep; Tampa is a dense downtown with hundreds of permitted facilities, dissolving bedrock, and a hurricane record. Between them they exercise every section the engine has, including the two conditional inserts, karst and the California hazard zones, which appear only where the ground calls for them. Neither address was chosen because it is unusual; both are ordinary houses on ordinary streets.

What to look for

  • The finding comes before the topic: each page's headline is a sentence with a number in it, not a label.
  • Every map has a collar: source, retrieved date, data vintage, projection, north.
  • Every code the report prints is explained once, in the codes key at the back, with a small raised k in the text pointing to it.
  • "Checks: passed" on the cover means every core source answered and every figure reconciled to its record. It is a retrieval and consistency check, not a verification of the public records themselves.
  • The samples were rendered under the working name Prelim on 13 October 2026. The Groundsheet imprint replaces it at launch; nothing else changes.
